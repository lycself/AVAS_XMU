# References

This page lists, by topic, the textbooks and papers cited in the primer chapters, each with a one-line note on what to read it for. Bibliographic entries give authors, title, publisher or journal, volume, page and year (pages are left out where uncertain); DOIs are not listed — search the publisher's site by title when needed. The chapters follow AVAS's actual behaviour; where it differs from textbook conventions, the "AVAS convention" callouts of each chapter say so.

For a first reading, start with Wangler's textbook: the primer follows its notation, and almost every chapter's "Further reading" points to the corresponding chapter of it. To get to the bottom of space charge and envelope theory, go on to Reiser; for systematic derivations of transverse linear optics and orbit correction, see Lee or Wiedemann.

## Textbooks {#references-textbooks}

- T. P. Wangler, *RF Linear Accelerators*, 2nd ed., Wiley-VCH, Weinheim, 2008. — The standard text on RF linacs: cavities and transit-time factors, longitudinal and transverse dynamics, the RFQ, space charge, the mismatch factor, halo, superconducting linacs; the primer follows its notation.
- M. Reiser, *Theory and Design of Charged Particle Beams*, 2nd ed., Wiley-VCH, Weinheim, 2008. — A systematic treatment of charged-particle beams: axially symmetric fields and Busch's theorem, periodic focusing and the smooth approximation, KV and rms envelope equations, space-charge-dominated beams, the Child–Langmuir law, equipartitioning, free energy of mismatch and emittance growth.
- S. Y. Lee, *Accelerator Physics*, 4th ed., World Scientific, 2019 (earlier editions exist). — A comprehensive textbook with clear treatment of transverse linear optics, Courant–Snyder theory, dispersion, orbit distortion and correction, and many exercises.
- H. Wiedemann, *Particle Accelerator Physics*, 4th ed., Springer, 2015. — A comprehensive reference (the 4th edition is open access) with systematic derivations of beam optics, phase space, magnets, dispersion and achromats, errors and orbit correction.
- A. W. Chao, K. H. Mess, M. Tigner, F. Zimmermann (eds.), *Handbook of Accelerator Physics and Engineering*, 2nd ed., World Scientific, 2013. — Handbook of formulas and data, for looking up common formulas and typical parameters.
- S. Humphries, *Charged Particle Beams*, Wiley, 1990. — An introduction to beam transport, focusing elements and space charge, well suited to self-study by graduate students.
- S. Humphries, *Principles of Charged Particle Acceleration*, Wiley, 1986. — An introduction to the principles of all kinds of accelerators, including electrostatic and RF acceleration and magnetic focusing.

## Linacs, RF and superconducting cavities {#references-linac}

- P. M. Lapostolle, A. L. Septier (eds.), *Linear Accelerators*, North-Holland, Amsterdam, 1970. — The classic collection on linacs, an original source of early design methods and beam dynamics.
- I. M. Kapchinsky, *Theory of Resonance Linear Accelerators*, Harwood Academic, 1985. — A systematic theory of longitudinal and transverse motion in resonance linacs.
- H. Padamsee, J. Knobloch, T. Hays, *RF Superconductivity for Accelerators*, Wiley, 1998 (2nd ed. Wiley-VCH, 2008). — Physics and technology of superconducting cavities: surface resistance, $Q_0$, $R/Q$, cavity shapes, peak fields and gradient limits; read it to understand the design constraints of HWR, spoke and elliptical cavities.

## Ion sources, low-energy transport and the RFQ {#references-injector}

- I. G. Brown (ed.), *The Physics and Technology of Ion Sources*, 2nd ed., Wiley-VCH, 2004. — Principles and extraction of the various kinds of ion sources.
- B. Wolf (ed.), *Handbook of Ion Sources*, CRC Press, 1995. — A handbook of ion sources, for typical parameters of the various kinds.
- I. M. Kapchinskii, V. A. Teplyakov, "Linear ion accelerator with spatially homogeneous strong focusing", *Prib. Tekh. Eksp.* No. 2, 19 (1970). — The original paper proposing the RFQ principle.
- K. R. Crandall, R. H. Stokes, T. P. Wangler, "RF quadrupole beam dynamics design studies", in *Proc. 1979 Linear Accelerator Conference*, Montauk, NY, 1979. — The representative paper on the adiabatic-bunching design of RFQs (shaper, gentle buncher, accelerator).

## Linear optics, elements and electromagnetic fields {#references-classics}

- H. Busch, "Berechnung der Bahn von Kathodenstrahlen im axialsymmetrischen elektromagnetischen Felde", *Ann. Phys.* 386, 974 (1926). — Busch's theorem: conservation of canonical angular momentum in axially symmetric magnetic fields, the starting point of solenoid focusing and Larmor rotation.
- E. D. Courant, H. S. Snyder, "Theory of the alternating-gradient synchrotron", *Ann. Phys.* 3, 1 (1958). — The founding paper of strong focusing: stability of periodic focusing, β functions and Twiss parameters, phase advance.
- K. L. Brown, "A first- and second-order matrix theory for the design of beam transport systems and charged particle spectrometers", SLAC-75, Stanford Linear Accelerator Center, 1967 (later revisions). — The origin of the TRANSPORT matrix conventions, the matrices of dipoles, edge angles and dispersion.
- K. Floettmann, "Some basic features of the beam emittance", *Phys. Rev. ST Accel. Beams* 6, 034202 (2003). — The relation between normalized emittance, energy spread and the definitions of emittance.
- H. Wollnik, *Optics of Charged Particles*, Academic Press, 1987. — Transfer matrices of electrostatic and magnetic elements.
- P. W. Hawkes, E. Kasper, *Principles of Electron Optics*, Academic Press, 1989 (later editions exist). — Systematic derivation of electrostatic lenses (including einzel lenses) and paraxial optics.
- J. D. Jackson, *Classical Electrodynamics*, 3rd ed., Wiley, 1999. — Linearity of Maxwell's equations and the superposition principle, Laplace's equation and boundary conditions; the electromagnetic basis of RF defocusing and of superposing fields.

## Space charge, envelopes, matching and halo {#references-space-charge}

- I. M. Kapchinskij, V. V. Vladimirskij, "Limitations of proton beam current in a strong focusing linear accelerator associated with the beam space charge", in *Proc. Int. Conf. on High Energy Accelerators and Instrumentation*, CERN, Geneva, 1959, p. 274. — The original paper on the KV distribution and the KV envelope equations.
- F. J. Sacherer, "RMS envelope equations with space charge", *IEEE Trans. Nucl. Sci.* NS-18, 1105 (1971). — Proves that the rms envelope equations hold for any elliptically symmetric distribution, the basis of rms matching and rms-equivalent beams.
- P. M. Lapostolle, "Possible emittance increase through filamentation due to space charge in continuous beams", *IEEE Trans. Nucl. Sci.* NS-18, 1101 (1971). — The definition of the rms emittance and emittance growth through space-charge filamentation.
- I. Hofmann, L. J. Laslett, L. Smith, I. Haber, "Stability of the Kapchinskij-Vladimirskij (K-V) distribution in long periodic transport systems", *Part. Accel.* 13, 145 (1983). — Instabilities of KV beams in periodic channels, one theoretical source of the rules for choosing the phase advance.
- J. Struckmeier, M. Reiser, "Theoretical studies of envelope oscillations and instabilities of mismatched intense charged-particle beams in periodic focusing channels", *Part. Accel.* 14, 227 (1984). — Envelope modes and the envelope instability, the origin of the $\sigma_0 < 90^\circ$ rule.
- M. Reiser, "Free energy and emittance growth in nonstationary charged particle beams", *J. Appl. Phys.* 70, 1919 (1991). — An upper estimate of emittance growth from the "free energy" of a mismatched beam.
- I. Hofmann, "Stability of anisotropic beams with space charge", *Phys. Rev. E* 57, 4713 (1998). — Space-charge coupling resonances of anisotropic beams and the stability chart (Hofmann chart).
- I. Hofmann, J. Qiang, R. D. Ryne, "Collective resonance model of energy exchange in 3D nonequipartitioned beams", *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 86, 2313 (2001). — Emittance exchange and the resonance model of 3D non-equipartitioned beams; read it to understand when the equipartitioning rule matters.
- R. L. Gluckstern, "Analytic model for halo formation in high current ion linacs", *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 73, 1247 (1994). — The analytic model explaining halo by particle–core resonance.
- T. P. Wangler, K. R. Crandall, R. Ryne, T. S. Wang, "Particle-core model for transverse dynamics of beam halo", *Phys. Rev. ST Accel. Beams* 1, 084201 (1998). — A systematic study of the particle–core model and of the relation between mismatch and maximum halo amplitude.
- K. R. Crandall, D. P. Rusthoi, *TRACE 3-D Documentation*, 3rd ed., Los Alamos report LA-UR-97-886, 1997. — Manual of the classic 3D linear envelope code, with its conventions for the mismatch factor, matching algorithms and element matrices; useful for comparison with AVAS's linear preview.

## Multi-particle simulation {#references-simulation}

- R. W. Hockney, J. W. Eastwood, *Computer Simulation Using Particles*, Adam Hilger, 1988. — Particle–mesh methods, charge assignment and numerical noise.
- C. K. Birdsall, A. B. Langdon, *Plasma Physics via Computer Simulation*, McGraw-Hill, 1985 (reprinted by IOP Publishing, 1991). — Numerical heating in particle simulations and the choice of mesh and time step.

## Errors and correction {#references-errors}

- T. O. Raubenheimer, R. D. Ruth, "A dispersion-free trajectory correction technique for linear colliders", *Nucl. Instrum. Methods A* 302, 191 (1991). — A representative paper on trajectory correction in linacs (for electron linear colliders), for the basic ideas of correction algorithms.
- J. A. Hanley, A. Lippman-Hand, "If nothing goes wrong, is everything all right? Interpreting zero numerators", *JAMA* 249, 1743 (1983). — The "rule of three" for an upper probability bound when no event was seen, for judging what "no seed showed losses" means in an error study.
- Linear propagation of errors, tolerances and one-to-one orbit correction: the corresponding chapters of Wangler, Lee and Wiedemann, and the estimates in the [errors and correction](#errors) chapter.

## In AVAS {#references-in-avas}

This section collects the AVAS conventions scattered across the chapters, unit and definition differences from codes such as TraceWin, and modelling rules of the multi-particle engine as a quick reference for readers already familiar with accelerator physics. Every entry links to the chapter where it is explained in detail.

### Coordinate, sign and phase conventions

| Topic | AVAS convention | Chapter link |
|---|---|---|
| 6D phase-space coordinates | $(x, x', y, y', z, \delta)$ with $x' = p_x/p_0$, $y' = p_y/p_0$, $\delta = \Delta p/p_0$; $z > 0$ means **ahead of** the reference particle (arriving earlier), with phase offset $\Delta\varphi = -2\pi z/(\beta\lambda)$ | [How AVAS describes a beam](#intro-reference), [Phase space and emittance](#phase-space-longitudinal) |
| Cross-section viewing direction | All cross-sections (beam spots, quadrupole / solenoid / steerer cross-sections) are viewed **from downstream looking upstream**, with the beam coming out of the screen ($\odot$), so $\boldsymbol F = q\boldsymbol v \times \boldsymbol B$ can be checked directly against the arrows | [Quadrupoles](#quadrupole-field), [Solenoids](#solenoid-field), [Steerers](#steerer-field) |
| Quadrupole gradient $G$ sign | For positive charges, $G > 0$ focuses horizontally ($x$) and defocuses vertically ($y$); reversed for negative charges | [Quadrupoles](#quadrupole-field) |
| Solenoid field $B$ sign | Focusing strength goes as $B^2$, independent of the signs of $B$ and $q$ (always focuses in both planes); the sense of the Larmor rotation angle $\theta = \int [qB/(2p)]\,\dd s$ follows the sign of $qB$ | [Solenoids](#solenoid-busch) |
| RF cavity time factor and phase | Electric field multiplied by $K_e\cos(\omega t + \varphi_0)$, magnetic field by $K_b\sin(\omega t + \varphi_0)$; synchronous phase for simultaneous acceleration and longitudinal focusing is $\varphi_s \in (-90^\circ, 0^\circ)$ (left of crest); standing-wave maps such as `hwr010` use $K_b = -K_e$ to align the $\boldsymbol E$ and $\boldsymbol B$ phases | [RF cavities](#rf-cavity-modes), [Longitudinal dynamics](#longitudinal-stability), [Field-map elements](#field-maps-in-avas) |
| RF cavity phase mode `V3` | `V3 = 0`: the 6th parameter is the synchronous phase $\varphi_s$ relative to the effective cavity centre (the engine solves for the entrance phase $\varphi_0$); `V3 = 1`: phase relative to the geometric centre; `V3 = 2`: absolute entrance phase $\varphi_0$ | [RF cavities](#rf-cavity-in-avas), [Field-map elements](#field-maps-in-avas) |
| Dipole magnets and path length | After a `bend`, the transverse coordinate $x$ points along the outward normal of the bending plane, and the longitudinal coordinate in the UI and `DataSet.txt` is the arc length $s$ along the reference orbit | [Dipoles and edge angles](#dipole-in-avas) |

|---|---|---|---|
| Element length `L` and aperture `R` | **Metres (m)** | TraceWin commonly uses mm; convert lengths and apertures to m when importing lattices | [How AVAS describes a beam](#intro-workflow), [Drift space](#drift-in-avas) |

### Engine rules and modelling checklist

1. **Do not place matrix elements directly at the very start or end of a multi-particle lattice**: placing matrix elements (`quad`, `solenoid`, `bend`, `edge`, `steerer`) as the first or last element of a multi-particle lattice can cause boundary/stepping issues in the engine; pad both ends with a short `drift` (e.g. 0.02–0.05 m; see [Multi-particle simulation essentials](#simulation-in-avas)).
2. **Keep `outputplane` more than $2\ \mathrm{cm}$ before the lattice end**: an output plane too close to the end of the lattice may not trigger output recording (see [Multi-particle simulation essentials](#simulation-in-avas)).
3. **Write `multithreading` as `1` in `input.txt` or leave it out**: the two are the same; 0, 2 or 3 make the engine lose all particles at the start (verified with both kernels). The thread count is set with “CPU threads” on the Settings page or `--threads`, for both kernels (see [Multi-particle simulation essentials](#simulation-in-avas)).
4. **A `steerer` must have length `0` and acts over the element immediately following it**: steerers take zero length in the lattice and superpose onto the next downstream element; giving a non-zero length shifts the geometry (see [Steerers](#steerer-in-avas)).
5. **`displacepos` in single-particle mode reads positions in mm**: mind the unit conversion when switching `input.txt` to single-particle trajectory mode (see [Errors and correction](#errors-in-avas)).
6. **Composite columns for centroids and longitudinal position in `DataSet.txt`**: without bends, the global longitudinal position $z$ is column 5 + column 33; the transverse beam centroids $x, y$ are column 29 + column 1 and column 31 + column 3 (see [Multi-particle simulation essentials](#simulation-in-avas)).
7. **Two historical quirks in the example project `examples/hwr010`**: (i) in `beam.txt`, `twissz` has $\beta_z = 0.37292\ \mathrm{mm/mrad}$ copied from `twissy` (which generates a Gaussian bunch with $\sigma_z = 1.316\ \mathrm{mm}$ and $\Delta W = 10.78\ \mathrm{keV}$), whereas the actual longitudinal statistics of `part_rfq.dst` are $\sigma_z \approx 1.24\ \mathrm{mm}$ and $\Delta W \approx 11.4\ \mathrm{keV}$ (see [Common beam distributions](#distributions-in-avas)); (ii) the `sol` field map contains point-by-point numerical noise, so the linear preview derives the radial fringe field from on-axis $B_z$ via Maxwell's equations to keep the transfer matrix symplectic (see [Solenoids](#solenoid-in-avas)).
